# Concepting

Phase 2 produces 3 distinct concepts. Distinct means different ideas, not different color schemes or variations of one idea.

## Where strong concepts come from

Look for an idea already inside the brand, so the site could only belong to this company:
- **The name or logo.** Can the mark become the signature moment? (A falling-figure logo becomes a figure that reacts to scroll speed.)
- **What the product does.** Can the site demonstrate it instead of describing it? (A voice tool whose decoration is live transcript text; a hardware product torn down part by part on scroll.)
- **The company's structure.** Can the navigation be the business model? (Divisions shown as rings or rooms you move between.)
- **A physical metaphor.** An architectural model, a darkroom, a publication, a keyboard key, a gallery wall, an art director's marked-up proof.
- **The user's vibe.** The words, materials, and moods from the intake vibe question are often the best seed for a concept. Use them.
- **The proof.** For a studio, showing how its own site is built is itself proof of skill.

The best concepts are ones where the trick and the evidence are the same thing.

## The ambition bar

At least one concept must be bold: the kind of idea a top studio would pitch to win the client, even if it is harder to build. For every concept, answer honestly: "Would this plausibly win Awwwards Site of the Day? Yes / maybe / no, because..."

Signs a concept is too safe:
- It is a familiar UI pattern with a clever label (a before/after slider, a theme switcher, a typing effect).
- It could be described in one sentence that also fits dozens of existing sites.
- Its signature moment is small, faint, or happens at the edge of the screen.

If all three concepts show these signs, discard them and think again before presenting.

## Scale the signature to the project

Decide this before concepting and record it in DESIGN.md:
- **Simple page** (waitlist, single landing page, one-pager): the signature is on the first screen. It can be still: striking type, a composition, an image, a material. Motion is optional, never required. If it does move, it plays without the visitor doing anything.
- **Journey site** (several sections or pages telling a longer story): the biggest moment can arrive later in the journey. The first screen must still carry a strong hook that clearly belongs to the same idea and makes people want to keep going, and the moment itself is reached by normal scrolling or navigation.

## The signature moment

One moment people would describe to a friend. It must:
- come from the concept, not be added on top
- be reached by every visitor without hunting for it: on a simple page it is on the first screen within about 3 seconds; on a journey site the first screen carries its hook and normal scrolling or navigation delivers it. Interactions (drag, hover, tap) can deepen it but must never be the only way to see it, because most visitors will never discover a hidden control
- be legible on a phone at normal reading distance
- if it moves, run at 60fps
- never block the main action or trap the user; if it plays once, offer a small replay control

## Honest risk check

For each concept, name the biggest way it could look generic or AI-made, and the biggest way visitors could miss it. Common traps:
- Blueprint grids, mono labels, and corner brackets (the default dev-tool look)
- Scroll-jacked "storytelling" that feels like a template
- A mascot that appears at many sizes and turns cute instead of premium
- Any decoration showing fake data or specs
- A signature moment hidden behind an interaction most people won't try

If the risk can't be avoided with a clear rule, choose another concept.

## Recommending

Recommend the concept that best serves the audience, the vibe, and the action they should take, not the most impressive demo. State the reason in one or two sentences tied to the intake.
