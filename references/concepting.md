# Concepting

Phase 2 produces 3 distinct concepts. Distinct means different ideas, not different color schemes of one idea.

## Where strong concepts come from

Look for an idea already inside the brand, so the site could only belong to this company:
- **The name or logo.** Can the mark become the signature moment? (A falling-figure logo becomes a figure that reacts to scroll speed.)
- **What the product does.** Can the site demonstrate it instead of describing it? (A voice tool whose decoration is live transcript text; a hardware product torn down part by part on scroll.)
- **The company's structure.** Can the navigation be the business model? (Divisions shown as rings or rooms you move between.)
- **A physical metaphor.** An architectural model, a publication, a keyboard key, a gallery wall.
- **The proof.** For a studio, showing how its own site is built is itself proof of skill.

The best concepts are ones where the gimmick and the evidence are the same thing.

## The signature moment

One interaction people would describe to a friend. It must:
- come from the concept, not be added on top
- work on touch devices if the audience is mobile (no hover-only or keyboard-only ideas as the core)
- run at 60fps
- be skippable or non-blocking; never trap the user

## Honest risk check

For each concept, name the biggest way it could look generic or AI-made. Common traps:
- Blueprint grids, mono labels, and corner brackets (the default dev-tool look)
- Serif headline turning italic halfway (the default premium-SaaS look)
- Scroll-jacked "storytelling" that feels like a template
- A mascot that appears at many sizes and turns cute instead of premium
- Any decoration showing fake data or specs

If the risk can't be avoided with a clear rule, choose another concept.

## Recommending

Recommend the concept that best serves the audience and the action they should take, not the most impressive demo. State the reason in one or two sentences tied to the intake.
